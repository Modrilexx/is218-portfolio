1. Research Question, Target Reader & Evidence Boundaries

Research Question: What specific technical, analytical, and business-communication proof points should an NJIT Business & Information Systems undergraduate highlight in a lightweight portfolio to compete for entry-level Data Analytics with Applied AI roles versus Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) paths?

Primary Target Reader: Analytics hiring managers, campus recruiters, and data team leads in financial services and enterprise technology reviewing candidates in 60–90 seconds.

Facts (From Employer Pages)

  • Financial services early-career programs (JPMorganChase) explicitly combine SQL/BI dashboards with generative AI and ML deployment.
  • Tech risk/analytics roles (Google) emphasize statistical problem-solving, stakeholder presentation, SQL/Python, and familiarity with ML systems.
  • FDE roles (Databricks) require deeper production cloud, Spark, and multi-year engineering experience plus travel.

Interpretations

  • A Business & Information Systems major has an immediate advantage on analytics + business-impact storytelling compared to pure theory portfolios.
  • Showing end-to-end project records (data model, validation rules, clear UI, and honest tradeoff notes) signals real workplace readiness.

Assumptions (To Validate)

  • Recruiters prefer fast-loading, scannable case studies and a direct résumé link over flashy animations.
  • Two lightweight layout options will let peer reviewers test whether a grid or split-sidebar layout makes key links faster to find.

3. Comparison of Two Career Directions

Primary Direction

Direction A: Data Analytics with Applied AI

Target Roles: Data & AI Analyst (Financial Services / Enterprise), Business Intelligence & Analytics Analyst, Product/Risk Data Analyst.

  • Strength Fit: Directly leverages my NJIT Business & Information Systems coursework, bridging stakeholder requirements, SQL/Python analytics, and practical AI tooling.
  • Entry-Level Accessibility: High—explicit early-career rotational programs (like JPMorganChase's Data & AI Analyst track) recruit undergraduates directly in the NJ/NYC metro area.
  • Portfolio Priority: Showcase structured case studies (problem → data/architecture → honest results), clean visual hierarchy, and verifiable repo links.
Secondary / Growth Direction

Direction B: Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) / AI Solutions

Target Roles: Associate Solutions Engineer, Deployment Strategist, and later Forward Deployed Engineer.

  • Strength Fit: Strong customer-facing communication and systems thinking, similar to consulting + implementation.
  • Barrier Right Now: Many FDE postings (like Databricks's) require 5+ years of distributed systems/Spark/cloud engineering experience.
  • Strategy: Keep FDE as a 3–5 year trajectory by building solid GitHub engineering fundamentals now while targeting Data & AI Analyst roles first.

4. Portfolio Design Decisions & Scaled Static-Site Plan

Two Brand & Layout Directions (Tested in Review 2 Draft)

  • Layout A — Executive Analytics Grid: Top navigation, compact KPI/skills strip, and a 2-column project card grid. Emphasizes rapid recruiter scanning.
  • Layout B — Editorial Split Sidebar: Fixed left profile/navigation rail with long-form case-study cards on the right. Emphasizes narrative depth and résumé readability.

Inspect both layouts live on the Review 2 Portfolio Draft →

Four Portfolio User Stories

  1. As a recruiter, I can identify Michael's major, target role, and GitHub/LinkedIn links within 10 seconds above the fold.
  2. As a hiring manager, I can scan 3 project case studies with clear role, stack, and status labels (including honest in-progress tags).
  3. As a recruiter, I can read a structured résumé draft directly on the page without downloading an unknown file first.
  4. As a peer reviewer, I can toggle between two layout options of the same content to give concrete usability feedback.

Scaled 6-Point Planning Checklist (Right-Sized for a Static Portfolio)

Checklist Area Portfolio Plan
1. Agile Work Plan Scope: Static HTML/CSS portfolio draft + career research site + résumé draft. Out of scope: blog CMS, contact backend, dark-mode cookie persistence.
2. Technical Architecture Pure static HTML5 + CSS3 + minimal vanilla JS (only for the Review 2 layout toggle), hosted free on GitHub Pages.
3. What I Do NOT Need (and Why) No database, no server, no user accounts, and no OOP design patterns. A personal portfolio is read-only public content; adding a backend or database would introduce maintenance and security surface for zero reader value.
4. Testing & Findability Checks Manual + peer findability check (can a reader locate work, résumé, and profile links immediately?), mobile viewport check (375px width), and link check verifying zero broken URLs. Status: Planned.
5. Code Quality & Accessibility Semantic landmarks (<header>, <nav>, <main>, <footer>), WCAG AA color contrast (≥4.5:1), keyboard-accessible layout toggle buttons, and HTML validation before publishing.
6. Build & Review Sequence Publish Career Research Site → publish 2-layout Portfolio Draft → run classmate peer review → log GitHub Issues → refine winning layout and insert final verified GitHub/LinkedIn links.
